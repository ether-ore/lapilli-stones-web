The game in thirty seconds

Simple rules. Unstable positions.

Lapilli begins like a familiar five-in-a-row game. Captures change everything: the line you are building can disappear in a single move.

01

Make five

Place five stones in an unbroken line — horizontal, vertical, or diagonal.

02

Take five pairs

Flank two enemy stones to remove them. Capture five pairs and the game is yours.

03

Hold the initiative

Create threats that demand answers. Defend the line and the capture at the same time.

Learn the basics in minutes. Spend years seeing what you missed.

An honest ladder

Four opponents. Four different conversations.

Not four labels on the same machine. Each rung is measured against the one below it and tuned to teach a different lesson.

INovice Sees the immediate danger, forgives the human one.
IIApprentice Looks past the move and begins collecting careless pairs.
IIIJourneyman Sees several replies and makes a blunder feel expensive.
IVArtisan Takes its time, reads deeply, and rarely returns the initiative.

An original Roman legend

Pompeii, 79 AD. The little stones that waited beneath a volcano.

Imagine a back room in Pompeii: green and amber drops of glass, a nineteen-line board, and a game of alignment and capture played while Vesuvius murmured above the city.

That is the legend of Lapilli — fiction, offered as fiction, and a frame for a real tradition of five-in-a-row and custodial-capture games.
